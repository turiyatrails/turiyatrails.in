Treks

Walk to notice, not to conquer

Five trails, graded honestly. These are the treks we know and lead ourselves, from a gentle forest climb to a glacier at the head of a valley.

Each trek lives inside a longer journey, and the shorter ones can also be walked on their own. Groups of 4 to 10, led by our BMC certified lead guide. Dates are released in small batches. Message us and we will tell you what is opening next.

Beginner · Kukuchina, near Dwarahat

Pandavkholi

A gentle forest climb to a high meadow tied to the years the Pandavas spent in hiding. The trail passes near the Mahavatar Babaji cave, and the meadow at the top is the kind of place people stop talking without being asked.

Trail
About 3.5 km each way, a steady forest path from Kukuchina village
Getting there
About 1.5 hours from Ranikhet via Dwarahat. We arrange the drive.
Suits
First-time trekkers who can walk a few hours

Part of: Into the Quiet, The Long Quiet

Day trek from Oakakhi: ₹3,500 per person

Intermediate · Near Kausani

Pinnath

A climb through oak and rhododendron to a temple ridge at 2,750 metres. The clouds usually arrive before you do, and on a clear day the high Himalaya stands in a line across the north.

Trail
About 5 km each way, a sustained climb
Altitude
2,750 metres. High enough to feel it.
Suits
Walkers comfortable going uphill for a few hours

Part of: Where the Clouds Rest

Day trek from Kausani: ₹4,000 per person

Challenging · Almora district

Bharatkot

The highest peak in its range, and the hardest walking near Ranikhet. Usually taken over two days with a night in camp on the mountain. It asks for fitness and gives back a sky you will not forget.

Trail
A full two-day trek with long distances and real climbs
Camp
One night on the mountain, equipment included
Suits
Fit, experienced walkers. We talk about readiness before confirming.

Part of: The Seeker's Ridge

Overnight trek: ₹9,500 per person

Moderate · Munsyari, Pithoragarh district

Khalia Top

Forest giving way to open high meadows above Munsyari, at around 3,500 metres. On a clear morning the Panchachuli peaks fill the horizon. Under snow in winter, flowering in early summer.

Trail
A long climbing day through forest to the meadows, with a camp night
Altitude
Around 3,500 metres
Suits
Walkers ready for five to six hours on the trail

Part of: Above the Treeline

Challenging · Bageshwar district

Pindari Glacier

A multi-day walk up the Pindar valley through the last villages, forest and moraine, to Zero Point at the foot of the glacier at around 3,600 metres.

Trail
Five walking days from the roadhead and back
Altitude
Around 3,600 metres at Zero Point
Suits
Fit walkers with some trekking experience

How we trek

Trekking as a form of inquiry

There is a version of Himalayan trekking that is about conquest: summits bagged, kilometres logged, photos taken. That is not us. We use the act of walking through mountains for something slower and harder to name.

  • A walking question each morning, with no obligation to answer out loud
  • A slow pace: 4 to 6 km a day on most treks, not 15
  • Simple, fresh food cooked for each meal
  • Guides trained in mindful facilitation, not performance
  • No competitive atmosphere. Everyone moves at their own pace.
  • Campfire conversations in the evening, never forced
  • Digital detox encouraged, not enforced
  • An honest conversation about readiness before you book
