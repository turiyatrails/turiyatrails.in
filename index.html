Not another adventure company

A journey can be one bird you finally see properly, an hour inside an oak forest, or a single long look at the night sky.

Every trek we found nearby was either too loud, too fast, or trying to sell us an adventure. Kalika forest, Oakakhi, the Kumaun skies, none of it needed dressing up. It needed someone to slow down enough to actually see it.

So a guide, an astro storyteller, a documentarian and a small homestay in Ranikhet built something plainer: walk slowly, watch closely, ask real questions, and let the mountains do the rest.

Our philosophy

Freedom is not running away. Freedom is seeing clearly.

The name comes from Turiya, a word from the Mandukya Upanishad for the awareness beneath waking, dreaming and deep sleep. Always present, rarely noticed. It shapes how we plan every day, in very practical ways.

What Turiya means

Less noise, more attention

Most travel is built around getting somewhere. Ours is built around noticing where you already are: a call in the canopy, the light changing on a ridge, your own breath on a climb.

Small groups, on purpose

Four to ten people, often fewer. Silence is easier to hold, the forest opens differently, and everyone is a person rather than a headcount.

Nature to experience, not just photograph

Cameras are welcome. But we plan days around what is worth being present for, not what photographs well.

Time to observe

Slow pace, unhurried mornings, and no rush to the next viewpoint. Four to six kilometres a day, not fifteen.

Respect for the hills and the people in them

Leave No Trace, always. Home-cooked Kumaoni food, local guides, and conversations with people who actually live here.

An old idea, in plain words

Turiya is the awareness beneath waking, dreaming and deep sleep. You do not need to believe anything to walk with us. You only need to be willing to slow down.

Questions

Before you message us

Is this suitable for beginners?

Yes. The Kalika Weekend and every day experience are built for beginners, and Into the Quiet is a gentle first trek. Our longer treks ask more, and we will be honest with you about which one fits.

Can solo travellers join?

Solo travellers are a large part of who we build these journeys for, not an edge case we tolerate. You can join a scheduled batch or a session on your own.

I am travelling from abroad. Can I join?

Yes, and you are very welcome. Our page for international travellers covers getting to Ranikhet from Delhi, visas, permits, seasons, money and what to pack. Prices are in rupees, with approximate US dollar prices alongside.

Where do journeys start?

Almost every journey starts at Oakakhi Stays, our home in Ranikhet. The nearest railway station is Kathgodam, about three hours away by road. Tell us how you are travelling and we will help you plan the last stretch.

How do I book?

Message us on WhatsApp or use the enquiry form. We talk first about what you are looking for, then share dates, the full plan and how to confirm your place. No booking is final until we have spoken.

What should I pack?

Layers, sturdy shoes, a headlamp, and less than you think. We send a full list once your dates are confirmed.
