Birdwatching

Learning to see a forest

The Himalayan forests around Ranikhet and Binsar host over 200 species of birds. Our guides teach you to hear the forest before you see it.

The experience

A living community, hiding in plain sight

Most people look at a forest and see trees. A birdwatcher looks at the same forest and sees a living community of hundreds of species, each with a role, a voice and a story. Our birdwatching mornings teach you to slow down enough to notice what has always been there.

The Kumaun Himalaya is one of India's richest birding regions, where the plains meet the mountains. We walk at Kalika near Oakakhi, on the Kasar Devi ridge above Almora, and at the hides by the Sattal lakes.

“The forest does not perform for you. You must become quiet enough that it forgets you are there.”

The Turiya Trails way
Residents of the Kumaun forests

Birds you may encounter

Great Barbet

Its loud, mournful call is the unofficial soundtrack of the Kumaun forests. Brightly coloured, usually heard high in tall trees before it is seen.

Khalij Pheasant

A forest pheasant often heard before seen. Its calls carry through the understorey at dawn around Ranikhet and Kalika.

Black Francolin

Heard in the early mornings around Oakakhi. A ground bird with a sharp, repeating call.

Verditer Flycatcher

A flash of blue at the forest edge, and one of the birds on our home list at Kalika.

Lammergeier

The bearded vulture rides thermals for hours over the ridges. One of the most dramatic birds of the Himalaya.

Himalayan Monal

Uttarakhand's state bird, found higher up between about 2,500 and 4,500 metres. A possibility on our mountain journeys, not at Ranikhet.

How it works

What a session looks like

  • An early start, 5:30 to 6am, when birds are most active
  • Walking in silence unless the guide signals
  • Birds identified by call before sight
  • Binoculars and a field guide provided
  • Two to three hours covering two or three kilometres, slowly
  • Tea afterwards and a look back at what was seen
  • No target list. Presence over checklist.
Where we go

Kalika forest and the oak forests around Oakakhi, the Kasar Devi ridge, and the Sattal lakes. On First Light, also Pangot and Kilbury.

Best season

March to June for summer migrants and breeding plumage. October to February for winter species. Each season is a different forest.

Group size

A maximum of six people. Smaller groups see more.
