Nature classes

Reading the forest

A small, hands-on class for anyone who has ever walked through a forest and wished they knew what they were looking at.

The experience

Names are the start of noticing

Once you can tell a banj oak from a chir pine, or a barbet's call from a cuckoo's, a forest stops being a green blur. It becomes a place full of particular things.

Our nature class is two unhurried hours at Oakakhi and in the edge of Kalika forest. You learn a few trees, a few calls, and how to keep a simple field notebook, so the next walk you take, anywhere, is a richer one. It suits curious adults and older children alike.

“You cannot protect what you cannot name, and you cannot name what you have never stopped to look at.”

The Turiya Trails way
What we cover

A first field course in two hours

Trees of the Kumaun forest

Banj oak, buransh, chir pine and the plants that grow in their shade. How to tell them apart, and why it matters which grows where.

Learning by ear

The calls you heard that morning, and how to start recognising birds before you see them.

The field notebook

A simple way to record what you notice: sketches, notes and questions. No drawing skill needed.

Reading signs

Tracks, feathers, feeding marks and the small clues that tell you who passed by.

How it works

What a session looks like

  • Small groups, so everyone can ask questions
  • Half indoors at Oakakhi, half at the forest edge
  • Notebooks and pencils provided
  • Suitable for adults and children aged about ten and up
  • Pairs well with a birding morning or astro night
Where

Oakakhi and the edge of Kalika forest, Ranikhet.

When

Late morning or afternoon, through most of the year.

Group size

Up to eight people.
