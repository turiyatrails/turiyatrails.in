Stargazing

The Himalayan sky as a doorway to wonder

At around 1,800 metres, away from city light, the Kumaun night sky is one of the darkest in north India. Stories written in stars, guided with intention.

The experience

The sky as a reminder of scale

Away from city light pollution, the night sky above Ranikhet is dark enough that on a clear, moonless night the Milky Way is not just visible, it is overwhelming. The kind of overwhelming that makes your problems feel appropriately small.

Our astro nights are not astronomy lectures. They are guided moments of stillness under the sky, where stories from different cultures about the same stars remind us that people have always looked up and wondered.

“You are not looking at the stars. You are looking back in time, at light that left its source long ago, arriving at your eye tonight.”

The Turiya Trails way
What you will see

Objects in the Himalayan sky

The Milky Way

A dense band of light across the sky on moonless nights. Our own galaxy, seen edge-on. The sight that stops every conversation.

Orion Nebula

A fuzzy star in Orion's sword to the naked eye. A stellar nursery about 1,300 light years away.

Jupiter and Saturn

Often visible, depending on season. Through binoculars you can see Jupiter's largest moons, the ones Galileo found in 1610.

Andromeda Galaxy

The furthest object visible to the naked eye, about 2.5 million light years away.

Shooting stars

Several an hour on any clear night, and many more during showers like the Perseids in August.

The Pleiades

Krittika in Indian astronomy, the Seven Sisters in Greek myth. Count how many you can see.

The sky depends on the weather. If clouds roll in, we move the session to another night where we can, or tell you honestly that it is not going to clear.

How it works

What a session looks like

  • Begins after dinner, around 8 to 9pm
  • A dark field or rooftop away from artificial light
  • Stories from Indian, Greek and other sky traditions
  • Binoculars for closer views
  • Hot chai under the sky
  • No phones. Just eyes and the sky.
  • A walking question shared before sleep
Where we go

The Oakakhi rooftop and open meadows near Kalika in Ranikhet, the Kasar Devi ridge, and on our Three Skies circuit, Kausani and Mukteshwar.

Best season

October to March for the clearest skies. The monsoon brings clouds, but the clarity after the rain in September can be extraordinary. We plan around new moon.

Group size

A maximum of eight people, so everyone can sit in comfortable silence.
